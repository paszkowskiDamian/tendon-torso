Each crank turns a spool, so its turns = joint angle × ratio. Every crank needs a ratchet or friction brake, or the arm will drop under its own weight.

How it works
  1. The servo turns the spool. Spool and drum are both r = 15.5 mm, so joint angle = servo angle.
  2. The flexor and extensor wrap in opposite directions. One winds on while the other pays out by the same length, so a single motor drives both ways and the loop never goes slack.
  3. Both run as straight vertical lines inside the hollow link: no pulleys, almost no friction.
  4. The bearings sit at the drum ends, so the middle is empty. The pass-through cord crosses the joint on its axis and keeps its length at any angle. That's how tendons for the elbow, wrist and gripper will travel down from the torso.
  1. Elbow (J1-S): a NEMA17 in the base winds the flexor and extensor on a 6 mm helical spool. The drum is 36 mm, so the motor turns 6× further than the joint and the joint gets 6× the torque.
  2. Wrist (J2): its flexor and extensor run inside two white PTFE tubes from a separate drive module, up the arm and through the elbow's open center.
  3. Each cord only slides inside its own tube, so bending the elbow bends the tubes without pulling the wrist cords. The two joints move independently.
  4. The drive module can sit anywhere. In the torso it becomes one slot in a row of motors.
  1. Every part here is printable CAD from the arm/ folder: hinge modules, roll modules, links, cranks and the shoulder beam. You can print them as shown.
  2. Hinges (shoulder flexion and abduction, elbow, both wrist axes): a hollow drum on two 608 bearings, wrapped by a pair of Dyneema cords. The tubes of the joints further out pass through the drum's open center.
  3. Roll joints (upper-arm rotation, forearm twist): a hollow rotor on two thin-section bearings. The cords come up from the tubes, turn 90° over small idler pulleys and wrap the rotor.
  4. Every joint gets its own colored pair of PTFE tubes. They run from its crank, through the shoulder beam, and down the core of every joint before it.
  5. Reach mode solves the inverse kinematics in the browser (the same method as arm/kinematics.py). Drag the gimbal arrows or the ball to move the hand; turn on orientation and drag the rings to twist it. The 7 joint angles and crank turns follow.
  6. Each crank turns a spool. A pin through the crank arm locks it every 10°, and its turns = joint angle × ratio. Later each crank can be swapped for a NEMA17 + spool.
