How it works
  1. The servo turns the spool. Spool and drum are both r = 15.5 mm, so joint angle = servo angle.
  2. The flexor and extensor wrap in opposite directions. One winds on while the other pays out by the same length, so a single motor drives both ways and the loop never goes slack.
  3. Both run as straight vertical lines inside the hollow link: no pulleys, almost no friction.
  4. The bearings sit at the drum ends, so the middle is empty. The pass-through cord crosses the joint on its axis and keeps its length at any angle. That's how tendons for the elbow, wrist and gripper will travel down from the torso.
  1. Elbow (J1-S): a NEMA17 in the base winds the flexor and extensor on a 6 mm helical spool. The drum is 36 mm, so the motor turns 6× further than the joint and the joint gets 6× the torque.
  2. Wrist (J2): its flexor and extensor run inside two white PTFE tubes from a separate drive module, up the arm and through the elbow's open center.
  3. Each cord only slides inside its own tube, so bending the elbow bends the tubes without pulling the wrist cords. The two joints move independently.
  4. The drive module can sit anywhere. In the torso it becomes one slot in a row of motors.
